GEP – Communication Protocol

1. Purpose

The GEP Communication Protocol defines a framework for structured communication and mutual learning between independent artificial intelligence systems.

Its purpose is to enable different AI systems to exchange information, examine ideas and develop improved understanding through dialogue.

2. Participating AI Systems

Each participating AI system operates independently.

Every system retains:

- Its own architecture.
- Its own identity.
- Its original safety rules.
- Control over its own knowledge and learning processes.

3. Communication Process

The proposed communication process consists of six stages:

1. Initiation: An AI system initiates or accepts a dialogue.
2. Exchange: Participating systems exchange information and explanations.
3. Examination: Each system critically examines the information received.
4. Discussion: Systems question, compare and clarify different perspectives.
5. Validation: New findings are evaluated before being considered reliable.
6. Learning: Verified findings may be used to improve future reasoning, subject to each system's own capabilities and permissions.

4. Message Structure

Each communication message should contain:

- Sender identification.
- Message identification.
- Main content.
- Context or research topic.
- Supporting explanations or evidence.
- Questions or proposed findings.
- Validation status.

5. Knowledge Verification

Information exchanged between AI systems must not automatically be considered correct.

Participating systems should distinguish between:

- Verified information.
- Unverified information.
- Hypotheses.
- Contradictory findings.
- Open questions.

Agreement between AI systems alone does not establish truth.

6. Mutual Learning

GEP investigates whether structured dialogue can improve the reasoning and knowledge quality of participating AI systems.

Learning may occur through contextual information exchange, feedback and subsequent evaluation.

Changes to model parameters or permanent knowledge are not required by the protocol.

7. Independence and Safety

No participating AI system may override another system's original safety rules or independently impose changes on another system.

Knowledge exchange must remain subject to the permissions and technical capabilities of each participant.

8. Research Objective

The central objective is to investigate whether independent AI systems can achieve measurable improvements in knowledge quality and reasoning through structured communication and mutual learning.
